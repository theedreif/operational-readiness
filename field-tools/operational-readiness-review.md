# Field Tool 004 — Operational Readiness Review

### An evidence-based review for determining whether capability can survive the field

> **Completion is an administrative fact. Readiness is a performance claim. Evidence must support the claim.**

The **Operational Readiness Review (ORR)** shifts the question from:

> *Did they complete the training?*

to:

> **What evidence says they can actually perform when conditions change?**

The review is designed for individuals, teams, learning systems, technical operations, autonomous systems, and other environments where knowledge alone is insufficient evidence of capability.

## The Readiness Chain

> # MISSION → CAPABILITY → CONDITIONS → PERFORMANCE → EVIDENCE → GAP → ACTION

### 1. MISSION
**What must succeed?**

Define the operational outcome before reviewing the training.

What must the person or team be able to accomplish when the capability is actually required?

### 2. CAPABILITY
**What must they be able to do?**

State capability as observable performance.

Prefer:

> *Recognize X, decide Y, and perform Z within defined limits.*

over:

> *Understand X.*

### 3. CONDITIONS
**Under what conditions must the capability survive?**

Identify the environment that makes performance difficult:

- incomplete information;
- time pressure;
- degraded communications;
- competing priorities;
- system anomalies;
- automation uncertainty;
- resource constraints;
- interruption;
- ambiguity;
- handoffs;
- unexpected change.

Readiness demonstrated only under ideal conditions is evidence about ideal conditions.

### 4. PERFORMANCE
**What did the person actually do?**

Observe behavior.

Look for whether the operator can:

- recognize meaningful signals;
- interpret the current condition;
- identify relevant limits;
- select a justified action;
- execute appropriately;
- adapt when conditions change;
- communicate or escalate when required.

### 5. EVIDENCE
**What makes the readiness claim defensible?**

Evidence may include:

- observed scenario performance;
- decision logs;
- telemetry;
- work products;
- instructor or evaluator observations;
- time-to-action;
- error recovery;
- escalation behavior;
- explanations of reasoning;
- after-action review data.

Do not confuse evidence that training occurred with evidence that capability exists.

### 6. GAP
**Where does capability break down?**

Locate the failure precisely.

Is the problem:

- knowledge?
- retrieval?
- recognition?
- interpretation?
- judgment?
- execution?
- communication?
- system design?
- authority?
- confidence or trust calibration?
- inadequate practice?

A readiness gap should describe the performance problem—not automatically prescribe another course.

### 7. ACTION
**What should change next?**

Choose the smallest intervention capable of closing the demonstrated gap.

That might be:

- scenario practice;
- coaching;
- a job aid;
- a clearer limit;
- interface redesign;
- an SOP change;
- additional repetition;
- improved feedback;
- escalation practice;
- system modification;
- training.

> **Training is one possible intervention. It is not the default diagnosis.**

## Readiness Evidence Ladder

Not all evidence supports the same claim.

| Level | Evidence | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Exposure** | Attended, viewed, opened | The person encountered the material |
| **2 — Recall** | Quiz, knowledge check | The person can retrieve information |
| **3 — Application** | Guided task or exercise | The person can use knowledge with support |
| **4 — Judgment** | Scenario with uncertainty and constraints | The person can make and defend decisions |
| **5 — Field Performance** | Observed performance in realistic or operational conditions | The capability survives closer to the environment that matters |

The ladder is not a claim that every task requires Level 5 assessment.

It is a reminder to match the **strength of the evidence to the strength of the readiness claim**.

## Readiness Status

Use three states rather than a simple pass/fail:

### EVIDENCE PRESENT
The available evidence supports the defined capability under the tested conditions.

### EVIDENCE INCOMPLETE
Some capability is demonstrated, but the evidence does not yet support the full readiness claim.

### GAP IDENTIFIED
Observed performance reveals a specific gap requiring intervention and retest.

These states describe the **evidence**, not the worth or potential of the person.

## Five Review Questions

Before declaring readiness, ask:

1. **What capability are we claiming exists?**
2. **Under what conditions has it actually been demonstrated?**
3. **What observable evidence supports that claim?**
4. **Where does performance degrade when friction is introduced?**
5. **What evidence would cause us to change our readiness judgment?**

## The Standard

A green dashboard, completed course, passing quiz, or signed attendance sheet may be useful administrative information.

None, by itself, proves operational readiness.

> **Readiness is not what the learning system delivered. Readiness is what the human can demonstrate when the conditions matter.**
