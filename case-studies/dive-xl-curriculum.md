# Subsea Autonomy — A Curriculum Brief for Extra-Large AUVs

> **Engagement status: Independent analysis / speculative proposal.** Written
> by Ed Reif from publicly documented information about extra-large autonomous
> undersea vehicles, including Anduril's Dive-XL and Ghost Shark, operating
> within the AUKUS partnership framework. No contract, no brief, no review or
> endorsement by Anduril Industries or any government. Nothing here is
> published on their behalf. See [PROVENANCE.md](../PROVENANCE.md).

## The situation, as publicly documented

Extra-large autonomous undersea vehicles are crewless platforms with long endurance and modular payloads. Routine navigation decisions can occur at the edge without continuous human supervision, while acoustic communications are constrained by the medium.

## The finding

The gap is not only a knowledge deficit. It is a reflex problem. Surface navigation, submarine, and aerospace-maintenance traditions each bring useful instincts, but none alone is sufficient for an autonomous submerged platform operating out of contact for long stretches.

## Six need domains

1. **Autonomy level and location of control** — from helmsman to mission architect.
2. **The authority line** — which decisions remain operator-held under degraded conditions.
3. **Operational environment and degraded comms** — scenarios designed around realistic connectivity.
4. **Safety assurance and risk** — state transitions as curriculum objects.
5. **Regulatory, compliance and partition** — releasability and delivery architecture.
6. **Cybersecurity, data governance and the human element** — including the transition from legacy operating assumptions.

## The measurement position

The primary programme metric proposed is **time-to-classify** — how long before an operator correctly names what they are seeing on a degraded feed.

Readiness is represented as a **Heat Map of Competence**, with the goal of distinguishing demonstrated judgment from simple completion.

Curriculum architecture for a platform class with no established training tradition, where the operator's job increasingly involves interpretation, the communications link may be unreliable by design, and authority boundaries matter.
