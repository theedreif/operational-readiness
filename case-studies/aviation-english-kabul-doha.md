# Language as Safety Equipment — Aviation English, Kabul and Doha

> **Engagement status: Employed engagement.** Raytheon Technologies. Aviation
> English instruction for Afghan military personnel, Kabul and Doha. DLI and
> ICAO certified. This page describes method only. It contains no personnel
> information, operational detail, or anything covered by contract or export
> control. See [PROVENANCE.md](../PROVENANCE.md).

---

## The situation

A flight line where the working language is somebody's second language, the
radio is the only channel, and the consequences of a misheard instruction are
not administrative.

ICAO sets a language proficiency standard for aviation because the industry
learned, expensively, that most of what gets called pilot error is a
communication failure wearing a different jacket. Teaching to that standard in
a training environment is one problem. Teaching to it in an active theatre,
where the convoy is late and nobody knows whether the delay is mechanical,
administrative or something worse, is another.

The students keep looking toward the door. That is the classroom you are
actually teaching in.

---

## The problem underneath the requirement

The contract was to raise measured English proficiency to a certification
standard. The operational requirement was different and larger: that a person
under stress, on a degraded radio, with an accent unfamiliar to the listener,
could make themselves understood **the first time**, in the four or five words
available.

Those two requirements are not the same requirement. A score improves with
vocabulary and test familiarity. Understanding under load improves with
something else: constrained phraseology, repetition under noise, and the
willingness to say *say again* to a superior.

Optimising for the first without the second is how a programme passes an audit
and fails a flight line.

---

## Approach

**Ambiguity treated as a hazard, not a style problem.**
The instructional target was not fluency. It was the elimination of the
specific ambiguities that kill: confusable numbers, conditional clearances,
negatives lost in static, politeness constructions that soften an instruction
into a suggestion. Clarity, taught as compassion — because the person you are
making yourself understood to is the one carrying the consequence.

**Phraseology under degradation.**
Practice was run against noise, speed and interruption rather than in clean
conditions. A phrase that only works in a quiet room is not a phrase. It is a
vocabulary item. This is the same principle later formalised as friction in
[Scenario Is the Product](../concepts/scenario-is-the-product.md) — the
difficulty is placed deliberately, at the point where the skill actually breaks.

**The four-word instruction.**
Compression as a competency. The operational form of a message is short,
unambiguous and survivable. The discipline of finding those four words later
became the shape of the
[45-Second Rule](../concepts/45-second-rule.md).

**Permission to interrupt.**
Any programme that raises proficiency but leaves a junior person unable to
challenge a senior one has not improved safety. Readback, challenge and
*say again* were trained as authorised behaviours with cultural weight
attached, not as classroom exercises. This is truth velocity, learned on a
flight line before it had a name.
→ [Operational Readiness](../concepts/operational-readiness.md)

## What it demonstrates

- Standards-based language instruction (ICAO, DLI) delivered in a contested environment
- Instructional design where the assessment standard and the operational requirement diverge, and both must be met
- Cross-cultural communication design under hierarchy and stress
- The origin of a doctrine that runs through everything after it: ambiguity is dangerous, clarity is compassion, language is safety equipment
