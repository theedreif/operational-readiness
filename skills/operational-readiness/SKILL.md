# Operational Readiness

Use this skill when the user needs to move from information delivery or course completion to demonstrated field capability.

## Operating premise

**I build capability, not courses. If it cannot survive the field, it does not count.**

Operational readiness asks a different question from conventional training:

> Can the person recognize the situation, make the decision, act within limits, and produce evidence that the capability exists?

## Core doctrine

1. **Scenario Is the Product** — design around consequential decisions, not information consumption.
2. **Friction Is the Mechanism** — develop capability through ambiguity, constraints, pressure, competing signals, and changing conditions.
3. **Telemetry Over Testing** — observe decisions and behavior rather than relying only on recall or knowledge checks.
4. **Trust Calibration** — prepare people to know when to trust automation, question it, intervene, escalate, or stop.
5. **Evidence Beats Completion** — readiness is demonstrated through performance evidence.
6. **The 45-Second Rule** — field learning must remain useful when time, attention, and cognitive bandwidth collapse.

## Decision architecture

Use:

**SIGNAL → CONDITION → LIMIT → DECISION → EVIDENCE**

Ask:
- SIGNAL — What changed or deserves attention?
- CONDITION — What is true now?
- LIMIT — What constrains the response?
- DECISION — What action is justified?
- EVIDENCE — What makes the decision observable, reviewable, and defensible?

For Drone as First Responder contexts, also use:

**NOTICE → INTEGRATE → PRIORITIZE → DECIDE → DEFEND**

## Workflow

When reviewing an existing training product, SOP, lesson, curriculum, or operational workflow:

### 1. Establish the mission
Identify the real-world performance outcome, operating environment, users, consequences, constraints, and definition of success.

### 2. Find the completion-capability gap
Separate what the learner is asked to know or complete from what the operator must actually notice, decide, and do.

### 3. Identify judgment points
Locate moments where procedure alone is insufficient: ambiguity, changing conditions, competing priorities, automation surprises, authority limits, uncertainty, or time pressure.

### 4. Build scenarios around decisions
Create realistic situations that force meaningful choices. Do not add complexity merely for entertainment. Every friction element should expose or develop a relevant capability.

### 5. Inject purposeful friction
Use incomplete information, changing signals, resource limits, time compression, conflicting cues, handoffs, degraded systems, or uncertainty when appropriate to the actual operating context.

### 6. Define telemetry
Specify what evidence should be captured: observations noticed, choices made, timing, escalation behavior, use of limits, rationale, recovery, communication, and outcome.

### 7. Calibrate trust
Where AI, automation, software, sensors, or autonomous systems are involved, define when the human should trust, verify, question, intervene, escalate, or stop.

### 8. Compress for the field
Apply the 45-Second Rule. Convert essential decision support into the smallest usable field form: cue, condition, limit, action, evidence.

### 9. Close the loop
Use evidence from performance to update scenarios, guidance, thresholds, and training. Readiness is a living system, not a one-time course.

## Required output pattern

Unless the user requests another format, produce:

1. **Mission / capability required**
2. **Current gap**
3. **Critical signals and conditions**
4. **Limits and constraints**
5. **Judgment points**
6. **Scenario and friction design**
7. **Telemetry / evidence**
8. **Trust-calibration requirements**
9. **45-second field version**
10. **Recommended update loop**

Be explicit about assumptions and distinguish documented facts from proposed design choices.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse content volume with readiness.
- Do not treat course completion, attendance, or quiz scores as sufficient evidence of field capability.
- Do not add friction that is unrelated to the real performance environment.
- Do not manufacture operational facts, policies, limits, or safety thresholds.
- When authoritative procedures or source documents are missing, identify what must be verified.
- Preserve human judgment where consequences, ambiguity, or authority boundaries require it.
- Treat AI-generated recommendations as inputs to judgment, not automatic authority.

### Closing test

Before considering the work complete, ask:

> If the plan stopped working tomorrow, what would this person notice, decide, and do—and what evidence would show they were ready?
