# Provenance Standard

The Evidence Locker distinguishes the relationship between Ed Reif and every example it contains.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Employed engagement** | Work performed as an employee or contractor, in role |
| **Client engagement** | Work performed under contract with a named client |
| **Independent analysis** | Work written on Ed Reif's own initiative from public information |
| **Speculative proposal** | Work prepared for a specific opportunity but not represented as adopted work |
| **Publication** | A publicly released authored or co-authored book, article, recording, presentation, or other creative work |
| **Public field evidence** | Publicly accessible material documenting field activity, experience, or context without implying ownership of the underlying event, organization, platform, or subject |
| **Lived-experience archive** | First-person or longitudinal public material documenting travel, place, adaptation, work, mobility, or other lived experience |
| **Lived-family evidence** | Public material documenting family or relational experience; it supports claims about the documented relationship and narrative, not professional engagement |

The purpose of this standard is simple: **separate evidence from implication**.

## Governing Rule

Evidence establishes only the relationship the source supports. Inclusion in the Evidence Locker does not by itself imply employment, endorsement, client status, authorship, ownership, exclusivity, adoption, or approval by a named organization.

Case studies should state what the work demonstrates, what Ed Reif's relationship to the work was, and any material limits on what can be publicly shown.

Publications should distinguish authored synthesis from the underlying people, organizations, technologies, places, historical events, and public information discussed in the work.

Lived-experience and lived-family archives are evidence of documented experience and synthesis. They are not automatically professional case studies.

Organisations, platforms, products and standards are named descriptively where relevant. Their names and trademarks remain the property of their respective owners.

## Traceability

**IDENTITY → CLAIM → CONCEPT → ARTIFACT → APPLICATION → EVIDENCE → PROVENANCE → UPDATE**

When a relationship changes or stronger evidence becomes available, update the provenance label rather than allowing implication to outrun evidence.
