# Ed Reif Field Toolkit v1.0

### Tools for Operational Readiness · Judgment Under Uncertainty · Decision Science

> **I build capability, not courses. If it cannot survive the field, it does not count.**

The **Ed Reif Field Toolkit** is a practical collection of compact tools for the point where plans meet reality.

The tools are designed to help people and teams **build capability, structure decisions, retrieve critical information, design realistic practice, evaluate readiness, shape exposure, and work around autonomous systems** when conditions are uncertain.

# How the Toolkit Works

The six tools are not separate ideas.

They form a field-learning and decision system.

### BEFORE THE MOMENT
Use the **Scenario Design Canvas** to create realistic practice.

Use the **45-Second Rule** to make point-of-need support usable.

Use the **Operational Readiness Review** to determine whether the capability claim is supported.

### IN THE MOMENT
Use the **Decision Field Card** to structure judgment.

Use the **DFR Judgment Card** when the environment involves autonomy, multiple information sources, and time-critical prioritization.

### WHEN STRUCTURING EXPOSURE
Use the **Asymmetry Check** to protect the floor, improve the odds, preserve options, and position for upside.

### AFTER THE MOMENT
Capture evidence.

Separate decision quality from outcome quality.

Identify the gap.

Update the system.

Retest.

> # PREPARE → NOTICE → DECIDE → ACT → EVIDENCE → UPDATE

# The Doctrine Behind the Tools

### Scenario is the product.
Practice decisions, not merely information consumption.

### Friction is the mechanism.
Capability must encounter ambiguity, constraints, pressure, and change.

### Telemetry over testing.
Observe what people notice, decide, and do.

### Trust must be calibrated.
Know when to trust the system, question it, intervene, escalate, or stop.

### Evidence beats completion.
A completed course is evidence of completion—not automatically evidence of capability.

# Use the Toolkit

These tools are intended to be compact enough to use in:

- scenario design;
- technical and operational training;
- coaching;
- simulations;
- tabletop exercises;
- performance support;
- autonomous-system training;
- after-action reviews;
- decision reviews;
- field learning.

They are frameworks for structuring thinking and learning. They do not replace applicable law, safety requirements, organizational policy, professional standards, command authority, or system-specific procedures.
